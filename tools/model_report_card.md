## 6. Strengths

- **Fluent, well-formed Urdu headlines**: low mean repetition (5.26%) and consistent ~14.4-word outputs that read like real newspaper headlines.
- **Strong lexical grounding**: 79.03% of summary content words are copied/inflected from the source — the model summarises *the given text*, not noise.
- **Heavy compression**: 20.4× mean reduction (up to 104.8×) while keeping headline-length outputs.
- **Decoding is already optimal**: the Task #15 sweep found no beam/penalty/length setting that beats the current config by more than +0.0001 ROUGE — quality is model-bound, not decode-bound.

## 7. Weaknesses

- **Faithfulness slips**: invention-rate 19.91% — the model sometimes asserts specifics (e.g. a body name or an event framing) not present in source or reference; number/entity preservation is imperfect.
- **Safe, generic vocabulary**: it favours high-frequency news words and its generic-headline prior, so rare/domain terms and precise word choice are often flattened (visible as low ROUGE-2 (17.15%)).
- **Focus selection**: on longer articles it can latch onto a secondary detail (e.g. a salutation or a sub-clause) instead of the reference's main event, which caps ROUGE-L regardless of fluency.

## 8. Verdict

The CoverageGRUSummarizer is **good for low-resource abstractive Urdu news summarization**: it reliably produces fluent, headline-length, well-compressed Urdu summaries whose content is largely grounded in the input (79.03% source-coverage, ROUGE-L 31.33%). Its **gaps are factual fidelity and rare-word choice** — a non-trivial invention-rate (19.91%) and a preference for safe, generic vocabulary mean specific names, numbers and precise terminology are sometimes approximated or dropped. **Decoding is already optimal** (Task #15), so the remaining head-room is in the model and training data — e.g. more/better-aligned pairs, richer vocabulary coverage, and faithfulness-aware training — not in inference-time parameters.
